## Concept-by-Concept Clone Audit

### CONCEPT 1: NEON UNDERWRITER (Insurance Risk Pricing)

**Clone Risk Score: 3/10**

**Most Similar Existing Game**: Fantasy sports apps (DraftKings/FanDuel) + Balatro's risk/reward tension. Secondary comparison: any game with "bet on yourself" mechanics (e.g., Inscryption's death card wagering).

**What a Steam reviewer would say**:

> "This is just Fantasy Football with auto-battler combat and a cyberpunk skin. You pick your fighters, set your expectations, and watch. I've been doing this on DraftKings every Sunday."

**What a game journalist headline would be**:

> "Neon Underwriter is basically DraftKings meets Super Auto Pets, and it's weirdly compelling"

**The 30-Second Test**: If you showed 30 seconds of the policy-setting phase, a gamer would think "some kind of betting game." If you showed the resolution phase, they'd think "auto-battler." Neither comparison is deadly, because neither captures what this actually is.

**Genuine Novelty Assessment**: The insurance underwriting framing IS genuinely novel. No game has made "pricing risk on outcomes you partially control" the core loop. The dual-layer decision (build the system AND bet on its behavior) creates a tension space that doesn't exist in fantasy sports (where you only draft, not build). The "Premium Adjustment" slider is the key differentiator -- you're not just betting, you're setting your own odds. This is legitimately new.

However: the execution MUST avoid looking like a gambling app. If the UI resembles a sportsbook, it's dead on arrival. The "insurance" framing needs to be front-and-center, not an abstraction over a betting mechanic.

**VERDICT: PASS (3/10)** -- Genuinely novel core mechanic. Risk is in marketing perception, not in mechanical cloning.

### CONCEPT 2: NEON BAZAAR (Adversarial Negotiation Economy)

**Clone Risk Score: 5/10**

**Most Similar Existing Game**: **Potionomics** (2022/2024). Potionomics literally turned haggling into a card-battle system with customer psychology. It received 80/100 on Metacritic and was commercially successful. Secondary: Recettear (shopkeeper RPG), Moonlighter (dungeon + shop hybrid).

**What a Steam reviewer would say**:

> "Isn't this just Potionomics flipped around? Instead of being the shopkeeper haggling with customers, you're the customer haggling with shopkeepers. Same energy, different perspective. Also the 'merchant memory' thing reminds me of Hades' relationship system."

**What a game journalist headline would be**:

> "Neon Bazaar flips Potionomics on its head -- now YOU'RE the one haggling, and it works"

**The 30-Second Test**: A gamer watching the negotiation UI would immediately say "shop game" or "Potionomics." The dialogue wheel would remind them of Mass Effect or Disco Elysium. The auto-combat resolution would make them think SAP/TFT.

**Genuine Novelty Assessment**: The SPEC-037 author claims "haggling roguelike" doesn't exist. This is wrong. **Potionomics already exists** and already turned haggling into a roguelike-adjacent card game. The key difference claimed -- "adversarial AI with memory and hidden agendas" -- is mechanically interesting but is an evolution of Potionomics, not a revolution. The counterfeit gear mechanic (gear that might be fake) is clever but feels like a layer on top of an existing concept, not a new concept.

The "Face" (mianzi) system adds cultural depth, but culturally-informed mechanics don't prevent clone perception. A reviewer who hasn't lived in Hong Kong won't appreciate the nuance -- they'll see "shop game with personality stats."

**VERDICT: CAUTION (5/10)** -- Potionomics is a direct ancestor. The concept is an evolution, not a revolution. Needs significant mechanical differentiation beyond "buyer instead of seller."

### CONCEPT 3: NEON TRIAGE (Consequence-Cascade Crisis Management)

**Clone Risk Score: 4/10**

**Most Similar Existing Game**: **Against the Storm** (2023) -- roguelike city-builder with cascading environmental disasters. Secondary: **Frostpunk** (moral triage under resource pressure), **80 Days** (route selection with cascading consequences), **FTL** (ship system triage during combat).

**What a Steam reviewer would say**:

> "This is FTL's event system mashed with Frostpunk's moral choices, compressed into 10 minutes. The 'which district to save' thing is literally the trolley problem repeated 12 times. It's compelling but not new."

**What a game journalist headline would be**:

> "Neon Triage brings Frostpunk's agonizing choices to mobile in bite-sized 10-minute runs"

**The 30-Second Test**: A gamer seeing the Kowloon district map with crisis icons would think "some kind of strategy game" or "Plague Inc." The cascade mechanic would remind them of Pandemic (the board game) where infections cascade to adjacent regions.

**Genuine Novelty Assessment**: The cascading interdependency system on a real-world map IS novel as a 10-minute roguelike. No existing game combines all three elements: (1) simultaneous multi-crisis triage, (2) persistent district interdependency, (3) 10-minute roguelike session. Against the Storm is the closest but plays out over hours, not minutes. FTL has triage but it's linear and isolated events.

The emotional core -- "I deliberately sacrificed Sham Shui Po" -- creates genuine narrative weight that most roguelikes lack. This could be the concept's killer feature.

The risk: the "sacrifice" mechanic could feel depressing rather than empowering. If every run ends with "look at all the districts you couldn't save," player retention suffers. The resolution needs to frame sacrifice as strategic heroism, not failure.

**VERDICT: CAUTION (4/10)** -- FTL + Frostpunk comparison is unavoidable but the compression into a 10-minute cascading roguelike IS new. Borderline pass.

### CONCEPT 4: NEON FORECAST (Prediction-Investment Coupling)

**Clone Risk Score: 3/10**

**Most Similar Existing Game**: **Wordle** (pattern recognition over sessions) + stock trading games + **The Cosmic Wheel Sisterhood** (fortune-telling aesthetic). No single game combines omen interpretation + resource investment + auto-combat resolution.

**What a Steam reviewer would say**:

> "It's like if Wordle had a baby with a stock market sim and that baby fought monsters. I can't really compare it to anything else, which is either genius or confusing. After 5 runs I'm addicted to decoding the omen symbols."

**What a game journalist headline would be**:

> "Neon Forecast turns fortune-telling into a roguelike, and we can't stop trying to read the signs"

**The 30-Second Test**: A gamer seeing the omen symbols would think "puzzle game" or "fortune telling app." The investment phase would look like no game they've played. The resolution phase would look like an auto-battler. No single comparison dominates.

**Genuine Novelty Assessment**: This is genuinely novel. No game in the entire landscape makes "interpreting ambiguous visual symbols and investing resources based on your interpretation" the core loop. The closest comparison -- Wordle -- is so different in genre and execution that no reasonable person would call this a clone.

The omen codex that persists across runs is a brilliantly sticky meta-progression mechanic. Players will WANT to fill it in, creating long-term retention that doesn't rely on power creep.

The risk: "too cerebral" for mass market. If the omen symbols are too abstract, casual players bounce. But the SPEC addresses this with clear early omens that gradually become ambiguous.

The Wong Tai Sin Temple / kau cim inspiration is authentic and adds genuine cultural identity. Fortune-telling is universal enough to translate across markets (NA astrology culture, JP omikuji, etc.).

**VERDICT: PASS (3/10)** -- No direct ancestor. Genuinely unprecedented mechanic. High confidence this passes the clone test.

### CONCEPT 5: NEON EDITOR (Narrative Weaponization via Editorial Selection)

**Clone Risk Score: 6/10**

**Most Similar Existing Game**: **Headliner: NoviNews** (2018). This is the elephant in the room. Headliner is literally a game where you are a newspaper editor choosing which stories to publish, and your choices affect society. It has faction influence, social consequences, and narrative weight. It sold well and was reviewed positively.

Secondary: **Reigns** (binary decisions affecting kingdom stats), **The Westport Independent** (2016, newspaper editorial sim under political pressure).

**What a Steam reviewer would say**:

> "This is Headliner: NoviNews with combat bolted on. The 'choose headlines to influence factions' thing has been done. Adding auto-combat doesn't make it new. The political angle is interesting but I've played this game before."

**What a game journalist headline would be**:

> "Neon Editor is Headliner: NoviNews meets Reigns, now with faction wars and neon"

**The 30-Second Test**: A gamer seeing the story selection + framing UI would IMMEDIATELY think of Headliner: NoviNews. This is the most dangerous comparison in the entire SPEC-037 lineup.

**Genuine Novelty Assessment**: The SPEC-037 author acknowledges Headliner as a reference point and claims the difference is "faction-driven auto-combat + roguelike." This is fair -- Headliner has no combat, no roguelike loop, no VS engines. But the core interaction -- "choose stories, frame them, watch social consequences" -- is identical to Headliner's core loop.

The faction combat layer IS genuinely additive. But adding combat to an existing concept is exactly the trap that SPEC-037's introduction warns about: "Existing Game X + roguelike wrapper." This concept does exactly what it criticizes: **Headliner + auto-combat roguelike wrapper**.

The Lennon Wall and Apple Daily references add cultural depth but also add political sensitivity that could complicate market entry in China (the #2 priority market per CLAUDE.md).

**VERDICT: FAIL (6/10)** -- Headliner: NoviNews is a direct ancestor. The Westport Independent is a second ancestor. Adding combat doesn't overcome the core clone perception. This concept violates SPEC-037's own stated principle.

### CONCEPT 6: NEON CATALYST (Sequential Chain Reaction Design)

**Clone Risk Score: 4/10**

**Most Similar Existing Game**: **Opus Magnum** / Zachtronics programming games (sequential I/O design). Secondary: **Rogue Voltage** (2025-2026, circuit-puzzle roguelike with chain combos), **The Incredible Machine** (Rube Goldberg physics puzzles), **Baba Is You** (rule manipulation puzzles).

**What a Steam reviewer would say**:

> "It's Opus Magnum simplified into a roguelike. Instead of programming alchemy machines, you're selecting compounds in sequence. The chain reaction animations are gorgeous but the core mechanic -- 'connect outputs to inputs' -- is Zachtronics 101. That said, making it selection-only instead of open-ended programming is clever."

**What a game journalist headline would be**:

> "Neon Catalyst brings Zachtronics-style chain reactions to mobile roguelike territory, minus the PhD requirement"

**The 30-Second Test**: A gamer watching the chain execution phase (sequential compound explosions cascading left to right) would think "some kind of chain reaction game" or possibly "match-3 variant." If shown the design phase (placing compounds in slots), they'd think "simplified programming game."

**Genuine Novelty Assessment**: The "selection-only chain building" IS meaningfully different from Zachtronics games. Opus Magnum gives you unlimited iteration on a spatial grid. This gives you random compound options and forces you to design a working chain from limited choices. The roguelike element (random offerings, growing chain length) adds real replayability.

**Rogue Voltage** (2025-2026) is the closest competitor: it's literally described as "a hybrid of roguelike deckbuilder and automation game" with "chain reaction combos." This is a legitimate threat. However, Rogue Voltage uses circuit boards and turn-based RPG combat, while Neon Catalyst uses chemistry and auto-resolving spectacle. The feel would be different enough.

The chain reaction spectacle (7 sequential explosions filling the screen) is the concept's strongest selling point. This would create incredible clips for social media / YouTube Shorts / TikTok. The visual payoff is genuinely compelling.

The "herbal medicine" and "wok cooking" cultural metaphors are clever but feel forced compared to other concepts' cultural integration.

**VERDICT: CAUTION (4/10)** -- Zachtronics comparison is inevitable but the simplification to selection-only + roguelike is a meaningful evolution. Rogue Voltage is a real competitor. Borderline pass.

### CONCEPT 7: NEON WARDEN (Population Curation as Ecosystem Management)

**Clone Risk Score: 3/10**

**Most Similar Existing Game**: **Papers, Please** (gate-keeping decisions) + **Super Auto Pets** (draft creatures with synergies). No single game combines "border control" + "ecosystem simulation" + "combat as emergent property of population balance."

**What a Steam reviewer would say**:

> "It's like Papers Please if instead of checking documents, you were building a terrarium. And the terrarium fights. I... don't know what to compare this to, actually. It's weird. I love it."

**What a game journalist headline would be**:

> "Neon Warden asks: what if Papers Please was an ecosystem sim? The answer is surprisingly addictive"

**The 30-Second Test**: A gamer seeing the gate decision UI (ADMIT/REJECT) would briefly think "Papers, Please." But once they see the living diorama ecosystem inside the zone -- creatures interacting, producing, hunting, collaborating -- they'd have no frame of reference. Nothing looks like this.

**Genuine Novelty Assessment**: This is the most genuinely novel concept in SPEC-037. No game combines these three elements: (1) gate-keeping immigration decisions, (2) predator-prey ecosystem as a living combat system, (3) 10-minute roguelike loop. The Papers Please comparison is superficial -- PP is about rule-following, this is about ecological design.

The "rejected arrivals become hostile" mechanic is brilliant -- it creates a consequence for rejection that doesn't exist in any draft game (in SAP/TFT, unpicked units just disappear). Here, everything you reject comes back to haunt you.

The ecosystem visualization (glowing diorama that brightens as it thrives) is unique and would be visually distinctive on any app store screenshot.

The Kowloon Walled City resonance is the deepest cultural integration of any concept -- a self-governing ecosystem in a walled enclave IS the historical Walled City. This isn't a metaphor slapped on top; the mechanic IS the cultural reference.

Risk: communicating the ecosystem rules clearly in a 10-minute mobile session. If players don't quickly understand predator-prey-producer dynamics, they'll churn. The SPEC addresses this with simple early gates, but execution is critical.

**VERDICT: PASS (3/10)** -- No direct ancestor exists. The combination is unprecedented. Papers Please comparison is surface-level only. High confidence this passes the clone test.

## Industry Pattern Analysis: What Selection Patterns Have NOT Been Used?

After exhaustive search, the following selection patterns have NO game using them as a core mechanic (as of March 2026):

1. **Auction Proxy** -- You don't bid directly; you set bidding rules for an AI agent who bids for you. Your selection is the strategy, not the bid.
2. **Triage Queue Reordering** -- A queue of tasks arrives; you can only reorder them, not add/remove. Priority is the mechanic.
3. **Confession/Lie Detection** -- NPCs tell you things; you select which claims to believe. Believing false claims poisons your strategy. No game has made credibility assessment the core.
4. **Menu Composition** -- You build a restaurant/bar menu (selection of offerings), and customer satisfaction drives combat power. Not Recettear (that's about pricing), but about curating the right mix for your audience.
5. **Signal Jamming** -- You see enemy communications and selectively jam/allow signals. The information warfare of "what do I let the enemy know" is unexplored.
6. **Time Budget Allocation** -- You have 60 seconds per wave to allocate across simultaneous tasks. Not action (no dexterity), but time-as-resource selection.
7. **Genealogy/Breeding Selection** -- Choose which creatures to pair across generations. Not Pokemon breeding (that's deterministic), but probabilistic trait inheritance as a roguelike gambling mechanic.

## The "Zero Comparison" Challenge

Can I think of a concept where NO existing game comparison is possible?

**Yes. Here is one:**

> **"NEON ARBITER"**: You are shown the aftermath of events (a destroyed building, a wounded NPC, a depleted power grid) and must select which PAST decision caused each outcome. You're reverse-engineering causality. If you correctly identify the cause, you can "rewind" that decision. If wrong, the damage spreads. The core mechanic is **forensic deduction applied to a branching timeline you didn't witness**.

No game has made "diagnosing causality in reverse" the core selection mechanic. Detective games (Return of the Obra Dinn, Her Story) reconstruct STORIES, not SYSTEMS. This would reconstruct CAUSAL CHAINS in a living simulation. The 30-second test would yield: "I have no idea what game this is like." That is the gold standard.

## Recommendation

### Concepts That Pass the Clone Test (Recommended for Round 4 Prototyping)

1. **NEON WARDEN** (3/10) -- Most genuinely novel. No ancestor. Strongest cultural integration. Highest emotional complexity. Recommend as **primary candidate**.
2. **NEON FORECAST** (3/10) -- Second most novel. Omen codex is a killer retention mechanic. Deepest meta-mastery. Strongest cultural authenticity (Wong Tai Sin). Recommend as **secondary candidate**.
3. **NEON UNDERWRITER** (3/10) -- Legitimately new mechanic space. Dual-layer decision is unique. Risk is in marketing ("insurance" is not sexy), but the gameplay tension is excellent. Recommend as **tertiary candidate**.

### Concepts to Revise (CAUTION)

4. **NEON TRIAGE** (4/10) -- Compelling but needs sharper differentiation from FTL/Frostpunk. Could be strengthened by emphasizing the 10-minute compression and cascading district interdependency as the unique selling point.
5. **NEON CATALYST** (4/10) -- Watch Rogue Voltage's market reception. If Rogue Voltage succeeds, this concept rides its wave. If Rogue Voltage fails, the "chain building" genre might be dead. Differentiate harder from Zachtronics.
6. **NEON BAZAAR** (5/10) -- Potionomics comparison is dangerous. Needs a mechanic that Potionomics doesn't have -- perhaps the counterfeit detection angle could become the primary mechanic instead of haggling.

### Concept to Kill (FAIL)

7. **NEON EDITOR** (6/10) -- Headliner: NoviNews exists. The Westport Independent exists. Adding combat to a newspaper editorial sim doesn't overcome the clone perception. Additionally, the Apple Daily / press freedom political angle creates market risk for China. Kill this concept.

### If None Pass: Direction for Round 4

If the CEO rejects all concepts, Round 4 should explore the **"Zero Comparison" patterns** listed above. Specifically:

- **Forensic causality deduction** (Neon Arbiter concept)
- **Signal jamming / information warfare**
- **Genealogy/breeding across roguelike generations**

These patterns have zero game ancestors and zero clone risk by definition.
